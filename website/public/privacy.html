Privacy

Yours stays yours.

Last updated 17 November 2026

Onyx runs on your PC. There’s no Onyx account, no analytics and no telemetry, and Onyx has no servers: nothing you say, play, type or receive is sent to us.

What Onyx collects

Nothing. Onyx has no servers, so it has nowhere to send your data. It doesn’t track how you use it and doesn’t phone home.

Voice typing

The microphone is used only while you hold your dictation hotkey, or between presses in hands-free mode.

Speech is turned into text on your PC by a speech model stored on your PC. The audio stays in memory and is never saved. The text is typed into the app you’re using and isn’t kept by Onyx.

If you turn on AI cleanup, the rewrite is done by a small language model running on your PC. Onyx talks to it over a connection that never leaves your computer.

Music

To show what’s playing, Onyx reads the song, artist and cover that Windows shares from media apps.

To tell music from videos, it also reads the titles of your browser tabs, and for YouTube Music, the song shown in the player bar. These are read on your PC and aren’t stored or logged.

The last song you played (title, artist, cover and which app played it) is saved on your PC, so the player can show it after a restart.

Gmail codes

Gmail codes is optional and off until you connect a Google account in Onyx Settings. When you connect, Google asks you to give Onyx read-only access to your Gmail (the gmail.readonly permission). Onyx can’t send, delete or change your mail.

What Onyx reads, and why. Onyx asks Gmail for new messages in your inbox, every 15 seconds while you’re using your PC, every 3 minutes when you’re away, and not at all on Battery Saver. It reads only messages that arrived in the last 10 minutes, and reads them only to find a one-time sign-in code. When it finds one, the island shows the code and the domain Gmail verified as the sender for a minute, so you can copy it.

Where your mail goes. Straight from Google to your PC, and nowhere else. Onyx has no servers, so your mail and codes are never sent to us or anyone else. Messages and codes are handled in memory only: they’re never saved to disk or written to logs. No person reads your mail. It isn’t used for advertising, isn’t sold, and isn’t used to train AI models.

What’s kept. Only the sign-in key Google gives Onyx, stored on your PC encrypted with Windows so only your Windows account can use it. When you copy a code, Onyx keeps it out of Windows clipboard history and the cloud clipboard.

Turning it off. Press Disconnect in Onyx Settings: Onyx deletes the key and asks Google to cancel its access. You can also remove Onyx’s access at any time from your Google Account, under Third-party apps & services.

Onyx’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your screen

In Dynamic mode, Onyx checks a thin ring of pixels around the island to see whether an app is using the top of the screen. Those pixels are checked in memory and discarded straight away. Onyx never takes or keeps screenshots.

System stats

The stats widget reads Windows’ own performance counters for CPU, GPU, memory, network and temperature. It reads them only while the widget is on, and keeps only the last few readings, in memory.

What’s stored on your PC

  • Your settings, in %APPDATA%\Islet
  • Downloaded models, the last song you played, and diagnostic logs, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Islet

The logs record app events and errors so problems can be fixed. They don’t contain what you dictate or the songs you play. Delete these folders to remove everything Onyx has stored.

When Onyx goes online

Onyx goes online only for these:

  • Downloading a speech model, from Hugging Face and GitHub
  • Downloading AI cleanup, from GitHub and Hugging Face
  • Lyrics: the title, artist and length of the song playing are sent to LRCLIB (lrclib.net) to find its lyrics
  • Covers for YouTube videos, from YouTube’s image server
  • Spotify, if you connect it: Onyx reads and controls what’s playing through Spotify’s Web API
  • Gmail codes, if you connect it, as described above
  • Pressing play on a song you last played in a browser that’s now closed, which opens a YouTube Music search for it in your browser

These services see an ordinary request from your computer, including your IP address, under their own privacy policies. Onyx sends them nothing beyond what’s listed here.

This website

This site sets no cookies and uses no analytics. If you interact with the island, official audio previews stream directly from Apple’s iTunes CDN. No embedded Spotify player or music-service account is used. It’s hosted on Firebase Hosting by Google, which keeps standard server logs, such as IP addresses, to run and protect the service. Fonts load from Google Fonts, animation scripts from cdnjs and unpkg, and some album covers in the demo island from Spotify, so those services see your request too.

Changes

If Onyx ever starts handling data differently, this page will be updated before the change ships, and the date at the top will change.
